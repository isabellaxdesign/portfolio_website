Case Study · Fidelity Investments

We replaced a PowerPoint with a board game for 180 people.

Fidelity's Workplace Innovation team needed something memorable for their annual summit. Four months, one fully illustrated game, and at least one person still using the cards at their desk.

Client Fidelity Investments
Timeline 4 months
My Role UX, Illustration, Facilitation
Tools Illustrator, InDesign
Scale 180 participants

The Problem

A 180-person summit was getting a PowerPoint.

Every year, Fidelity's Workplace Innovation team gathered their people for a summit of executives, designers, and project managers all in one room. And every year, the format was the same: a presentation and a group worksheet.

The ask was vague but exciting: do something fun and interesting. The real challenge underneath that? How do you hold the attention of 180 people while actually instilling values and behaviors that stick beyond the event?

Before

After

My Role

UX designer, illustrator, and first-time facilitator.

I owned this project end-to-end. That meant bringing my game design background into a professional context for the first time, thinking through how many spaces should be on the board, what the card mechanics should feel like, how to synthesize complex content into something playable.

I leaned into Fidelity's existing frog metaphor to build the game's world, which became Hop-Portunity, a swamp-themed game where teams navigate financial and workplace challenges together.

Most importantly, I designed and facilitated a pilot test with real clients before the live event. It was the first time I'd run a client workshop in that capacity, and it's the part of this project I'm most proud of.

Process

Four months from concept to a room of 180 players.

1

Discovery & content synthesis

Worked with the Fidelity team to understand the values and behaviors they wanted to instill. Translated dense content into game mechanics that could live on cards and spaces.

2

Game design & UX decisions

Made intentional design choices at every level: board size, number of spaces, card types (Finance, Resource, Disruptor), scoring system, frog character system. Every decision tied back to the experience goal.

3

Illustration & production

Built the entire visual world in Illustrator: board, cards, game pieces, scoreboard, rulebook, box. Leaned into the Fidelity frog brand metaphor to create a cohesive, lovable aesthetic.

4

Pilot testing with clients

Facilitated a full pilot workshop before the live event. It was my first time running a client workshop in that capacity, and I used feedback to refine mechanics and timing before the 180-person event.

5

Live event execution

Ran the game simultaneously across all tables for 180 participants: executives, designers, and PMs playing together in real time.

The Work

A fully illustrated game world, built from scratch.

Every asset was designed and illustrated in Illustrator, from the board to the box to the live scoreboard screen.

The Outcome

A summit people actually remembered.

The event was a success by every measure: engagement, energy, and execution. But the most meaningful outcome came after the room cleared.

"At least one person is still using the game cards in their everyday work, months after the event."

That's the whole point of experience design. Not just a good day, but a behavior that stuck. The game did what a PowerPoint never could: it made the content personal, competitive, and memorable.
