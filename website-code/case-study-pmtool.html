Case Study · Collective Next

Cutting through the noise of a super busy PM tool.

A vibe-coded internal tool with a real UX problem: too much information, not enough clarity. Redesigning it to serve two audiences without overwhelming either one.

Client Collective Next (Internal)
Status In Progress
My Role UX Design, Figma
Tools Figma, Replit AI
Users PMs + Clients

The Problem

A tool built fast, with a design that shows it.

The Collective Next Project Planner was vibe-coded, built quickly with AI to solve a real operational need. It works. But the design is super busy, and the information architecture doesn't reflect how PMs actually think about projects at a high level.

The core challenge: the tool has two distinct audiences with different needs. Internal PMs need a command center. Clients receiving project timeline links need clarity and confidence, not a wall of data.

🗂️

Internal PMs

  • Bird's-eye view of all projects
  • Task status at a glance
  • Team availability and assignments
  • Stage gates and deadlines
🔗

Client-facing links

  • Project timeline clarity
  • Progress without internal noise
  • Clean, trustworthy presentation
  • No login required

Before & After

Same information. Way less noise.

The original design surfaces everything at once: every task, every detail, every status. The redesign is about synthesizing what matters at the bird's-eye view and letting PMs drill down when they need to.

Key Insight

What I learned about designing with AI.

This project taught me something I hadn't fully articulated before: AI needs to be briefed like a junior designer, not a search engine. The quality of a Figma implementation via AI is entirely dependent on how well you've specified the design intent upfront.

Understanding what MCP servers need to execute a Figma design (the constraints, the component logic, the naming conventions) changed how I think about design handoff entirely. Not just for AI, but for human engineers too.

💡

The unlock

"The better you understand what AI needs to execute your design, the better your design specs become, for AI and humans alike."
